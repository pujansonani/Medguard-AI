Why MEDGUARD AI?
Existing ICU scoring systems (SOFA, SAPS II, APACHE) are derived from fixed tabular data obtained at admission. MEDGUARD AI extends this by integrating continuous 24-hour telemetry streams (18 variables) with contemporaneous, unstructured clinical notes via a cross-modal attention architecture. This enables the model to capture evolving patient trajectories that point-in-time scores miss.
Intended Use
This tool is intended as a supplementary clinical decision support aid for ICU clinicians. It is designed to flag patients at elevated risk of acute deterioration early enough to allow proactive intervention. The score should always be interpreted in the context of the full clinical picture and cannot substitute for clinical judgment.
Model Outputs
The primary output is a calibrated 48-hour mortality probability (0–100%). Secondary outputs include epistemic uncertainty (via Monte Carlo dropout), multi-horizon risk trajectories at 6h, 12h, 24h, and 48h, and feature-level SHAP attribution values to explain individual predictions.

Clinical Pearls & Pitfalls
  • 1 Lactate is the strongest individual driver. Even mildly elevated lactate (2–4 mmol/L) substantially increases the predicted risk. Trending lactate over time (rather than relying on a single value) often better reflects the patient's trajectory.
  • 2 MAP < 65 mmHg is a critical threshold. The model assigns a large positive SHAP contribution when MAP drops below 65 mmHg, consistent with the physiological definition of septic shock. Vasopressor use adds an additional multiplicative effect.
  • 3 Clinical note language matters. Phrases like "goals of care", "comfort measures", or "worsening" in clinical notes are detected by the BioBERT module and elevate the predicted risk — reflecting real-world documentation practice around deteriorating patients.
  • 4 GCS adds non-linear signal. The model is especially sensitive to GCS drops below 10. If GCS decline is gradual, consider checking for new structural causes (stroke, mass lesion) alongside metabolic derangements.
  • 5 Use SHAP drivers to guide workup. If the model's top driver is "Creatinine" rather than vitals, this may prompt an earlier nephrology consult or fluid strategy reassessment before hemodynamic decompensation occurs.
Model Architecture
Risk Score (48h) = σ( α·GRU(timeseries₁₈) ⊕ β·BioBERT(notes) ⊕ γ·XGB(tabular) ) Where: GRU(·) = Temporal GRU encoder (24h window, 18 physiological streams) BioBERT(·) = Fine-tuned Bio_ClinicalBERT on MIMIC discharge summaries XGB(·) = Gradient-boosted tree on 47 engineered clinical features ⊕ = Gated cross-modal attention fusion σ(·) = Sigmoid calibration layer (temperature-scaled) α, β, γ = Learned modality-weighting coefficients Output: Calibrated P(mortality within 48h) ∈ [0, 1] Epistemic uncertainty via 30× Monte Carlo dropout Multi-horizon heads: P(mort | 6h), P(mort | 12h), P(mort | 24h), P(mort | 48h)
